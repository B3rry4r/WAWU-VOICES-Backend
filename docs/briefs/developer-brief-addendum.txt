This addendum extends the existing developer brief. Where it conflicts with the earlier
brief on these five topics, this addendum applies.


1. News tags: three levels
Every post that carries news or reports of events must show exactly one of three tags.


  Tag                            Meaning                                    Who can apply it

  Verified News                  Confirmed by WAWU's editorial team         Admin and editorial
                                 against a named source. A source link is   accounts only
                                 attached.

  Unverified                     A claim of news that has not been          Poster, or admin
  News                           confirmed.

  Gossip                         Talk, rumour, opinion or entertainment.    Poster, or admin
                                 Not presented as fact.


Requirements

     The tag is chosen by the poster at the time of posting. Users cannot select Verified
     News. Only admin and editorial accounts can.
     Each tag has a distinct colour and label that is always visible on the post, in the feed, in
     search and when shared outside the app.
     Admin can change a post's tag at any time. Every change is logged with who, when and
     why. The poster is notified if their tag is changed.
     Users can filter and follow the feed by tag (Verified News only, Unverified News,
     Gossip, or all).
     Reports and flags work on tags. If a post is flagged as misleading, it goes to the admin
     review queue.
     Shared links and cards show the tag so the label travels with the post.
     A short, plain-language disclaimer explains each tag. This text should be reviewed by
     counsel as part of the legal pack.

Acceptance: a post cannot be published without a tag; a normal user cannot mark a post
Verified News; the tag is visible wherever the post appears.

2. AI weekly report from the polls
Use the weekly Resolution Polls, together with that week's answers and reactions, to
produce a published report on what Nigerians are saying and feeling.

Inputs

     The week's poll results (question, options, votes, and breakdowns by state or region
     where available).
     All text and voice answers posted under that week's theme. Voice answers are
     transcribed first.
     Emoji reactions and engagement on the week's questions.

What the AI produces

     Top themes and the main complaints, grouped and counted.
     Overall sentiment and the leading emotions (for example anger, anxiety, hope,
     sadness), with the movement from the previous week.
     The poll result and what it says people want fixed.
     A small number of anonymised, representative quotes or voice clips (only with the
     user's consent or where the terms allow it).
     A plain-language summary a journalist or brand could use, plus the underlying charts.

Rules

     The report is drafted automatically after the poll closes and is reviewed and approved
     by an admin before it is published. Nothing is published unreviewed.
     The report is labelled as AI-assisted and human-reviewed.
     No personal data. Anonymous posts stay anonymous in the report. Do not report on
     groups too small to be anonymous.
     Published weekly on the web and inside the app, with a shareable card and a
     downloadable PDF. Past reports stay in an archive.
     The system must handle English, Pidgin and, where possible, the major Nigerian
     languages in transcription and analysis.

Acceptance: each week has one report in a draft state ready for admin approval; once
approved it appears in the archive and can be shared.


3. Advert placements: two types
There are two separate advert products, each clearly labelled to users.

3A. Survey adverts from brands
A brand pays to ask the community a survey or poll.

     The brand submits questions (poll, text or short voice answer) and picks who should
     see it: categories, states, time period.
     It appears in the feed as a Sponsored Survey with the brand name visible.
     Responses are aggregated. The brand receives a results report. The brand never sees
     individual identities, and anonymous users stay anonymous.
     Admin approves every survey before it goes live (tone, legality, brand safety).
     Optional: the AI summary tool from section 2 can produce the brand's report.
     Pricing and packages are set by admin (for example per survey, per response target, or
     per day live).


3B. Product adverts
A brand or individual pays to promote a product, service or page.

     Placement options: a feed card, a banner, and a short audio ad slot. Ads appear
     between posts and never inside a user's post.
     Each ad is labelled Ad, supports a link or a call-to-action button, and can be targeted by
     category and state.
     Basic metrics for the advertiser: impressions, taps and listens.
     Admin approves every ad before it goes live and can pause or remove an ad at once.


Shared requirements
     A simple ad manager in the admin area: create, schedule, approve, pause, report.
     Brand-safety controls so that admin can stop ads from appearing beside sensitive
     categories.
     Clear difference between a Sponsored Survey, an Ad and a normal post, so that users
     are never misled.


4. Daily override of a post
Admin must be able to replace the featured question or post for one day, and the normal
flow must continue afterwards.

Requirements

     Admin can choose any day (immediately, or scheduled) and set content to appear in
     place of that day's scheduled question. Content can be text, voice, an image, or a link.

     The override runs for 24 hours (start and end time are editable). When it ends, the
     question flow resumes automatically.
     The question that was replaced is not lost. It moves to the next available slot, and the
     rest of the schedule moves forward by one day. Admin can also choose to drop it
     instead.
     The weekly theme and the Resolution Poll are not changed by an override unless admin
     chooses to change them.
     An emergency option applies the override immediately and, if chosen, sends a push
     notification.
     Every override is logged (who, what, when, what it replaced).
     Admin can cancel an override early, and the schedule returns to normal.

Acceptance: after an override ends, the next scheduled question appears without manual
work, and the replaced question is still in the queue.


5. Pricing: one-off tokens for extra voice length
Voice posts have a default length. Users who want to talk longer can buy extra length as a
one-off token.


  Token                                       Maximum voice length   Price

  Extra voice, 30 seconds                     up to 30 seconds       ₦150

  Extra voice, 1 minute                       up to 1 minute         ₦200


Requirements

     These are one-off purchases. They are not a subscription and do not renew.
     One token covers one message only. To post another long message, the user buys
     another token.
     The first 10 posts (text and voice combined) remain free. This token applies to extra
     voice length beyond the default.
     The user sees the price and what they get before paying, and can buy the token at the
     moment they hit the length limit while recording.
     Purchases show in the user's history with receipts, and the amounts are recorded for
     reporting.
     Prices must be configurable in the admin area so that they can be changed without an
     app update.

Part 2: Sharing and Interface Flow
Sections 6 and 7 were added after the first five requirements. Together they are the
current addendum to the developer brief.


6. Sharing a post: the recipient must understand it at a glance
When someone shares a VOICES post (a question, a single voice, a poll result or a weekly
summary), the person who receives it must understand the argument immediately. They
should not need the app, an account, or time to listen to everything.

Every shared post must show three things

 1. The key topic. The question in one clear line, with the country and the week's theme.
 2. The arguments and where it is headed. The two or three main positions in plain words,
     with how many voices sit behind each. If a poll is open, show the current lean as "so
     far", never as a final result. If people have proposed next steps, show how many
     proposals there are and which has the most support.
 3. An immediate call to action to lend their voice. One large button: Add your voice.

The share card (the preview that appears in WhatsApp, X, Instagram, iMessage, SMS
and email)

     Generated automatically from the post's data as an image plus preview text.
     Contents: topic line, two or three argument chips, a small poll bar or "N voices so far",
     the news tag if the post has one (Verified News, Unverified News or Gossip), the
     VOICES logo, and the call to action.
     Text and brand only. No user photos or user images.
     Four versions: shared question, shared single voice (shows the opening lines of the
     transcript), shared poll result, shared weekly summary.
     Sized correctly for each platform's preview, and written in the sender's language.

The share page (what opens when the link is tapped)

     Works in a mobile browser with no app and no account. The visitor can read the
     argument summary, read the transcript, play the featured voice, and see the poll "so
     far".
     Add your voice lets the visitor record straight away in the browser, or opens the app at
     the same topic with the recorder ready. An account is only asked for at the moment of
     publishing.
     If the visitor installs the app from the link, they land back on the same topic (deferred
     deep link).
     The news tag and its label stay visible, so an Unverified News or Gossip post is never
     read as fact.

Rules for the summary on the card and page

     Written by AI from the topic's voices and poll results, and labelled "Based on N voices"
     with the time it was last updated.
     Balanced: when there is an opposing argument, the leading view and the main
     opposing view both appear.
     It never claims agreement. Use wording such as "most voices so far lean towards..." or
     "people are split". Never "everyone agrees".
     Featured and weekly topics are reviewed by an editor. Ordinary posts are generated
     automatically with guardrails: no personal data, no quoted threats or abuse. If a post is
     flagged, its card falls back to the topic and the call to action only.

Measure: shares, link opens, taps on Add your voice, recordings started, and voices
published from a shared link.

Acceptance: a person with no account opens a shared link and, within a few seconds, can
say what the topic is, what the main arguments are, and can start adding their own voice.



7. Interface flow and setup
Design and implement the interface flow for VOICES, a public conversation platform that
launches in Nigeria and is built to work in any country. Keep the experience simple,
intuitive and engaging.


7.1 The main conversation flow
Every topic follows four stages, in this order:

Speak → Discuss → Vote → What Next?

     A stage bar showing all four stages is visible on every topic screen, so a user can move
     between them at any time.

     Language rule: the word "Rant" must not appear anywhere in the interface. That
     includes buttons, headings, notifications, share cards, emails, onboarding and app
     store text. Use "voice", "question", "topic" and "conversation" instead. Existing copy
     that says "Today's Rant" or "Join the rant" must be renamed (for example "Today's
     Question" and "Add your voice").


7.2 Home screen
     Topic of the Week, shown prominently.
     The daily featured question.
     Trending and new conversations.
     Clear buttons to Speak, Listen, Discuss and Vote.
     Simple bottom navigation with four items: Home, Discover, Speak, Profile.
     Notifications and messages sit as icons in the top bar. The Newsroom (the three news
     tags) lives inside Discover.


7.3 Speak
     Users can respond with a voice recording or text.
     A clear flow: record, preview, retake, publish.
     Optional prompts that help users express their thoughts, powered by AI Credits.
     Credits are bought by the user and are never required to speak.
     Before recording, the screen clearly shows how much recording time is available (free
     time, and any extra time bought as a one-off token from section 5) and what any credit
     or token costs. There are no surprise charges.


7.4 Discuss
     Voice and text contributions are shown under the original question.
     Users can reply directly to a contribution, by voice or text.
     Users can show agreement, disagreement, or share a personal experience, each as a
     clear option.
     The design encourages meaningful discussion without restricting natural expression.


7.5 Vote
     Show the polls that are relevant to the conversation.
     The user selects an option and submits their vote.
     Show the actual poll results and vote counts.
     The user can explain their choice (by voice or text), or go back to the discussion.

7.6 What Next?
     Users can suggest solutions, ideas or practical next steps.
     Other participants can support, challenge or improve each proposal.
     Similar proposals are grouped where practical. Grouping can be suggested
     automatically and confirmed by a moderator.
     An editorial summary shows the main arguments, the poll results and the proposed
     next steps.
     Do not present unverified proposals as proven solutions. Label every proposal as
     proposed, show how many supported or challenged it, and never imply a discussion
     has reached consensus when it has not. The summary states how many voices and
     votes it is based on.


7.7 Product rules
     Join any stage. Users can enter Speak, Discuss, Vote or What Next? without
     completing the others first.
     No forced form. A visitor can start speaking before filling in a multi-step form.
     Recording and preview work first, and sign-in is asked for only at the moment of
     publishing.
     Keep recording and participation simple.
     Allow strong opinions and disagreement, while moderating harassment, threats,
     doxxing and other harmful conduct.
     Do not reward outrage alone. Ranking and discovery must favour meaningful
     contributions: voices that are listened to through, that draw replies, that add a new
     point or a personal experience, that are marked helpful, and that bring in different
     viewpoints. Reaction counts, and angry reactions in particular, must not be the only
     ranking signal.
     Responsive, accessible and easy on mobile. Meet WCAG 2.1 AA, with automatic
     transcripts for every voice, screen reader labels, scalable text, touch targets of at least
     44 pixels, and good performance on slow networks. Recordings made without a
     connection are saved and sent when the connection returns.


7.8 Acceptance checklist
     The four stages are reachable from any topic and in any order.
     The word "Rant" appears nowhere in the product.
     A new visitor can record, preview and retake a voice before creating an account.
     Available recording time and any cost are shown before recording starts.
     Poll results show real counts, and a voter can explain their vote.

     Proposals are labelled as proposed, and no screen implies consensus.
     Every voice has a transcript, and the interface passes an accessibility review.
